Prize and reward scams commonly tell recipients that they have won money, vouchers, phones, competitions, lotteries, or other valuable rewards.

Unexpected prize messages are particularly suspicious when the recipient did not enter a competition.

Scammers may ask the recipient to click a link, call a premium number, pay a processing fee, provide bank details, or reply with personal information before receiving the supposed prize.

Words such as congratulations, winner, urgent, claim now, free prize, cash reward, and limited time are commonly associated with promotional and scam messages, although the presence of these words alone does not prove that a message is fraudulent.

Recipients should independently verify unexpected prizes through the organisation's official communication channels before taking action.
